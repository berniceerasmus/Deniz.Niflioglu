The purpose of private English time

Deniz already receives a lot of English at school. This lesson is for retrieval, repetition, speaking confidence and independent answers. The aim is not to give him harder English for the sake of it; it is to help him use the English he is learning more independently.

Today's targets

  • Answer the familiar warm-up questions independently.
  • Recognise and use the months of the year more confidently.
  • Understand the jobs of am / like / can.
  • Read a longer, age-appropriate passage and find information.
  • Speak in short complete sentences without immediately looking for reassurance.
  • Use English through movement and games, not only written exercises.

Ask → Think → Answer

Give Deniz 3–5 seconds before helping. If he looks to Mum, use: “Think first. You know this. Tell me.” Praise independent attempts even when they are not perfect.

Useful praise

“Great thinking!” • “You remembered that by yourself!” • “Good try — fix it and say it again.” • “Excellent! No help needed!”

7 MINMONTHSMOVEMENT
Use the month cards in the Games tab. Cut them apart. Mix them up. Deniz must put the months in the correct order. Then remove 2 cards and ask him which months are missing.

Round 1 — Build the year

Teacher: Put all 12 month cards face up on the table and mix them. Say, “Start with January.” Deniz finds January and then builds the rest of the year in order. When finished, he reads all 12 months aloud.

Round 2 — Missing Month

Teacher: Ask Deniz to close his eyes. Remove 2 month cards. Say, “Open your eyes. Which two months are missing?” Deniz names the missing months, finds where they belong, and puts them back.

Round 3 — Before, After & Birthday

Teacher: Point to any month and ask one question at a time: “What month comes before March?” or “What month comes after July?” Then ask, “What month is your birthday in?” Deniz answers and then tells you the month before and the month after his birthday month.

8 MINGRAMMARSPEAKING

When do we use AM?

Use am after I when you say what you are, how old you are, or how you feel.

Example: I am seven. / I am happy.

When do we use LIKE?

Use like after I when you talk about something you enjoy or prefer.

Example: I like computers. / I like apples.

When do we use CAN?

Use can after I when you talk about an ability — something you know how to do.

Example: I can read. / I can swim.

7 MINGAMESPEAKING
Use the sentence cards and the large AM / LIKE / CAN cards in the Games tab. This is a movement-and-speaking game: Deniz moves, chooses, and then says a complete sentence.

Round A — Find & Read

Teacher: Place 6 sentence cards around the room. Say, “Find a card!” Deniz walks to one, reads it aloud, and brings it back.

Round B — Make it about you

Deniz reads the card and changes one part so the sentence is true about him. Example: “I like books.” → “I like computers.”

Round C — Listen, Choose, Speak

Teacher: Put the big AM, LIKE and CAN cards on the floor. Say one prompt, such as “happy”, “computers” or “swim.” Deniz walks to the correct card, then says a full sentence: “I am happy.” / “I like computers.” / “I can swim.” Give 1 point for the correct card and 1 bonus point for a full sentence.

Round D — Deniz becomes the teacher

Deniz chooses a prompt card for you. You make a sentence. Deniz listens and says “Correct!” or “Try again!” if the wrong word was used.

Keep it playful

Use a point for an independent answer. Use a bonus point when Deniz corrects himself without help.
