What these lessons are for

Deniz already hears a lot of English at school. These private lessons are different. They are his practice, memory and confidence space: a place to use the English he already knows, say it out loud, and feel proud of it.

Our goals for Weeks 1 and 2

  • Remember and use everyday school English
  • Answer simple questions by himself, without looking at you first
  • Say complete short sentences
  • Follow 2-step and then 3-step instructions
  • Talk about himself, school, what he likes and what he can do
  • Read a short text and show that he understands it

This page is for you. The Week and Homework tabs are written so that Deniz and Mum can use them together.

🗣️ Words

School and computer words such as computer, book, pencil, bag, school, teacher, friend, and action words such as read, write, play, go, learn.

The full word list and the picture game are in the Memory Game tab.

💬 Sentences about himself

🧩 Grammar

  • Week 1: I am… / I like… / I can…
  • Week 2: I play → he plays. Also: Do you…? and Can you…?

See “Grammar in plain words” below for simple explanations.

👂 Listening and doing

Deniz listens to English instructions and does them in order: “Pick up your pencil. Put it on the table. Open your book.”

📖 Reading and speaking

He reads a short story, answers questions about it, and then speaks by himself for 30–60 seconds about his school day.

Every lesson follows the same pattern. When Deniz knows what is coming, he can put all his energy into the English.
  1. Hello and chat (5 min): Deniz answers simple questions about himself.
  2. Memory Blast (5 min): he remembers words from last time, using pictures.
  3. New language (10 min): one small piece of grammar, step by step.
  4. Game or movement challenge (10 min): for example, following “coding” instructions.
  5. Speaking mission (10 min): Deniz speaks by himself for about a minute.
  6. Deniz Challenge (5 min): a short challenge and a confidence point.

Lessons last about 45–50 minutes.

The “Think first” routine

When you ask Deniz a question, wait a few seconds. If he looks at you for help, smile and say:

“Think first. You know this. Tell me.”

Please do not give the answer. His own answer, even with mistakes, is the goal.

Homework: 5–10 minutes

  • Sit with Deniz, but let him do the typing and writing.
  • He says his sentences out loud to you.
  • Short and happy is better than long and stressful.

Praise the effort

  • “Great! You answered by yourself!”
  • “I like how you thought about that.”
  • “You didn't need any help!”
  • “Excellent try — keep going.”

Please try not to

  • Correct every small mistake
  • Finish his sentences for him
  • Translate straight away

How it works

  1. Deniz looks at a picture. The word is not shown.
  2. He says the English word out loud.
  3. He types the word in the box under the picture.
  4. He presses Check my answers. Green means correct. Orange means look again.
  5. He fixes the orange ones and presses Check again, or presses Start again for a new round.

Tip

Five minutes a few times a week works better than one long session. If he is stuck, say only the first sound (for example “b…”) instead of the whole word.

🔎 I am / I like / I can

Three small starters that let Deniz talk about himself.

I am seven. (who I am)
I like computers. (things I enjoy)
I can read. (things I am able to do)

After “I can” we use a simple verb: I can read, I can write.

🔎 He plays, she reads

When we talk about one other person (he or she), most verbs get an -s at the end.

I play → he plays
I read → she reads
I go → he goes (a few verbs add -es)

🔎 Do you…? / Can you…?

These are the questions Deniz learns to ask and answer.

Do you like school? → Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
Can you read? → Yes, I can. / No, I can't.

🔎 Where? What? Who?

Question words help Deniz find the answer in a story.

Where → a place (at school)
What → a thing (a pencil)
Who → a person (his friend)

Deniz makes mistakes. Should I correct him?

Not every one. At this stage the most important thing is that he speaks with confidence. The teacher corrects the important patterns gently in the lesson.

He looks at me or switches to Turkish.

Smile, wait about five seconds and say “Think first.” Most of the time he does know the answer.

He cannot remember a word.

Show the picture again and give the first sound. Only say the whole word if he is still stuck, and then ask him to repeat it.

How long should homework take?

5–10 minutes. The aim is remembering and saying, not lots of writing.

8 MINLISTENINGMOVEMENT

📝 How to do it

Goal: Listen carefully and do the steps in the right order.

  1. The teacher says a short algorithm. Deniz listens once.
  2. Deniz does the actions in the same order.
  3. Level up: add a third step.
  4. Reverse the code: Deniz is the programmer and gives the teacher two or three instructions. The teacher follows them exactly.

Say: “Touch your head.” “Point to the door.”

✅ Success: Deniz does a 2-step algorithm correctly, then tries 3 steps.

🔎 Command words

To tell someone to do something, start with the action word. We do not say “I” or “you”.

Touch your head.
Pick up your pencil.
Sit down.

Add “please” to be polite: “Open your book, please.”

🔎 Things and actions

English has two big kinds of words. Things are called nouns. Actions are called verbs.

Things: pencil, bag, computer → I have a pencil.
Actions: read, play, write → I can read.
